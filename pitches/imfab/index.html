01

Lead-Gen Gap Analysis

Measured against how modern industrial B2B buyers actually evaluate and select a fabrication partner.

GAP A

Conversion path is one generic contact form

A purchasing engineer with a print in hand wants to upload a drawing and get a number — not fill in "tell us about your business." Today that path doesn't visibly exist; quoting is buried inside a general Contact page with no RFQ-specific flow, file upload, or persistent CTA.
  • No sticky/persistent "Request a Quote" in the header or as a floating action — it competes with Jobs and Contact as just another nav link.
  • No file-upload RFQ form for DXF / STEP / PDF drawings, the standard entry point on modern fab-shop sites.
  • No mid-funnel offer (capability statement PDF, spec sheet download) for buyers not ready to quote yet.
GAP B

Trust is asserted, not demonstrated

ISO 9001 and "Made in America" exist as text on isolated pages, not as proof a buyer carries through the whole evaluation.
  • No certificate image/PDF, no case studies, no client or industry logos, no named testimonials anywhere in the crawl.
  • No project portfolio — a new buyer can't see tolerances held, materials worked, or finished-assembly photography before calling.
  • "Family-owned since 1974" is a real differentiator against larger, less personal competitors — it's mentioned, never built into the story.
GAP C

Structured by machine, not by buyer intent

The nav assumes the visitor already speaks fabrication fluently. It has no answer for a buyer who searches "agriculture equipment fabricator Missouri" or "defense metal fab supplier" rather than "punching and shearing."
  • Zero industry-specific landing pages, despite equipment (heavy plate, large-format laser, robotic welding) that clearly targets ag, construction/heavy equipment, defense, and OEM production work.
  • The "one source shop" claim — the actual differentiator — isn't shown as one continuous production flow anywhere; it's implied by having separate, disconnected pages.
GAP D

No content layer for top-of-funnel search

No blog, resources, or process-explainer content — the highest-leverage, lowest-cost channel for capturing engineers researching a process before they're ready to name a supplier.
  • No "waterjet vs. laser," material-selection, or tolerance-guide content to rank for research-stage queries.
  • Capacity data (tonnage, wattage, bed size) exists but isn't in a scannable, structured, or downloadable format — engineers fact-check specs fast; today they'd have to read prose per page.
02

Information Architecture

The redesign keeps every real capability on the current site — nothing is dropped — but regroups it around two forces that have to coexist on every page: the One Source Shop promise (breadth, proven as one continuous flow) and Made in America credentialing (trust, proven with documentation, not adjectives).

One Source Shop

Structural — becomes a Capabilities hub that visually chains Design → Cut → Form → Machine → Weld → Finish → Ship, so breadth reads as one integrated process instead of a dozen disconnected machine pages.

Made in America

Persistent — ISO 9001 + domestic-sourcing badges live in the header/footer on every template, plus a dedicated Quality hub with the actual certificate and documentation, not just a claim.

Industries We Serve

Find your industry — Agriculture, Construction & Heavy Equipment, Defense & Government, Energy, OEM.

Quality & "Made in America"

Credential, documented

ISO 9001 certified since 2001. Every part built on American soil, by American hands.

  • Certificate image (not just a badge icon), a two-line quality-policy excerpt, and a short "why domestic sourcing matters" note — links to the full Quality & Certifications hub.
07

Featured Project

Proof-by-example
  • One flagship case study: challenge → process (which capabilities were used) → result, with real part/assembly photography. Links to the full Our Work library.

Why I&M

Differentiation, scannable
  • Family-owned since 1974 — three generations of accountability, not a rotating account team.
  • 325,000 sq ft, one campus — no subcontracted hand-offs between processes.
  • 100+ combined years of AWS-certified welding experience on the floor.
  • Short list, not marketing prose — a buyer should be able to scan this in five seconds.
